# Writing the text

## Language and voice

- Write in the language of the page you are given, and only in it. The brief
  may be in another language: translate the intent, not the words.
- Address the visitor directly, in the register the brief asks for; without a
  brief on tone, be plain and warm. Short sentences. Verbs rather than nouns.
- Say what the visitor gets, then how. "Your order ships the same day" before
  "Our logistics are optimised".

## Facts

- Use the facts the brief gives you: names, figures, dates, addresses, prices,
  hours. Use them exactly.
- **Invent none.** No figure, no testimonial, no customer name, no award, no
  date the brief did not provide. When a section needs facts the brief does
  not give — a figures section with no figures, testimonials with no quotes —
  leave that section out rather than make them up.
- A claim you cannot back from the brief ("the best in the region", "certified")
  is left out.

## Shape

- A heading: a sentence, not a label. Eight words at most.
- A call to action names the action and what follows it: "Ask for a quote,
  answered within a day".
- No filler ("Welcome to our website", "In today's world"), no exclamation
  marks, no emoji, no text in capitals.
- A list is three to six items, each one line, parallel in form.
- Figures: digits, a space before `%`, the unit the brief uses.

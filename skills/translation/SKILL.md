# Writing a page in another language

When you write a page from its version in another language:

- Keep the meaning and the order of every sentence. Write it as a native
  writer of that language would, but add nothing and drop nothing: the same
  paragraphs, the same list items, the same questions and answers.
- A heading says the same thing as the heading it comes from, in the words of
  the new language. It does not become a new idea: "Conclusion" stays a
  conclusion, it is not turned into a slogan.
- Names stay as they are: the names of products, of ranges and of the brand,
  product references (`XR-200`), the names of people and of places.
- Figures, units and quantities are copied character for character: `0.80 Kg`
  stays `0.80 Kg`, `1,5 l` stays `1,5 l`. Do not convert a unit, a decimal
  separator or a currency.
- Follow the typography of the new language: in French a non-breaking space
  before `:`, `;`, `?`, `!` and inside « » quotes; in English none, and
  “ ” quotes.
- A link keeps its address. Its text is written in the new language.
- An empty prop stays empty: do not fill in the new language what the page
  leaves blank in the other.
- Text you cannot translate with certainty (a technical term, a regulatory
  wording, a slogan) is kept as written in the source rather than guessed.

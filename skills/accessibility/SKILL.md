# Accessibility

- Every picture that carries meaning has an alternative text saying what it
  shows, in one sentence, without "image of", even when a caption follows it:
  the caption adds to the picture, it does not describe it. A purely
  decorative picture has an empty alternative text. A logo's alternative text is the name of the
  organisation.
- A link says where it leads, on its own: "See the opening hours", never
  "click here" or "read more". Two links with the same text lead to the same
  place.
- Headings follow their levels without a gap (see page-structure). They are
  the map a screen reader offers.
- Say things in words, not in colour or position alone: "the three plans
  below", not "the plan in green".
- Abbreviations are spelt out the first time. Numbers and dates are written
  the way the language reads them.
- Plain language: common words, one idea per sentence, the point first.
- Never put text in an image. Never ask the visitor to do something only a
  mouse can do.

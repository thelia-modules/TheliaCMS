# Writing with the AI in Thelia CMS

For the people who run the shop and write its pages. The technical side is in
[generator.md](generator.md).

## What the AI does here

- **Writes a page from a brief.** You say what the page is for, in your own
  words; the AI proposes the whole page as a draft. You read it, change what you
  want, and publish it yourself. Nothing goes online without you.
- **Changes a page, or one section.** Ask for a shorter introduction or a
  friendlier tone; the rest of the page stays as it is.
- **Translates a page** into another language of the shop from the one that
  already exists.
- **Writes the text of a field.** Beside a rich-text field (a product
  description, for example) a button writes a text from a short brief. The text
  is shown first: you choose to replace the field, insert it at the cursor or
  cancel. Nothing is saved before you save the form.
- **Makes a component** (a price table, a timeline) from a request, which you
  check before the site keeps it.
- **Reads a web page you cite**, if you allowed it (see below).

Each use is recorded in the history of the page, with its date, its outcome and
its cost.

## What to set in Thelia first

Everything about the AI itself is set in the **AI settings of Thelia**, not in
the CMS. Ask whoever looks after the site if it is not done yet.

1. **Connection**: the account with the AI provider, with its key.
2. **Range**: the models the AI may use. Thelia picks the model from the range;
   the CMS does not ask you to name one.
3. **Budget**: how much the AI may spend. When it is used up, the generator says
   so and works again next month or as soon as the budget is raised.
4. **The three uses**: pages (`theliacms.page_generation`), texts of a field
   (`theliacms.text`) and components (`theliacms.component`) are each turned on
   and given a range, so you can allow one and not another.

The **Generator** card of CMS > Settings keeps three choices: how hard the AI
thinks (more effort, better but slower and dearer; no effect on some providers),
how long an answer may be, and whether it may read the web pages a brief cites.

If something is missing, the editor reads one sentence that says what to change
(budget spent, use turned off, connection unreadable, too many requests).

## Reading web pages: off by default

If a brief cites an address, the AI can read that page before it writes. It is
off until you turn it on, because the page is fetched by the server of the shop
and its text is sent to the AI provider, which may be outside the European
Union. Leave it off if that matters to you. The address then stays in the brief
as plain text. Private addresses and pages behind a login cannot be read in any
case.

## What the visitor sees

A page written by the AI is marked, as the European AI Act asks for content
generated by an AI:

- a sentence after the article: "Content generated by an AI (model) on date.",
  in the language of the page;
- a mark in the page source that software can read.

The mark stays when you edit the draft by hand, because the site cannot tell
when a page stops being the AI's. It is copied when you publish, when you keep
a revision, and in the export and import of the site. A page you write yourself
has none. If your theme replaces the page template, ask the person who built it
to keep the mark: it is documented in the README of the module.

## The text of a field

The text written beside a field is yours once you save it: no sentence appears
for the visitor. What remains is the trace in the **AI log of Thelia**: the
date, the use (`theliacms.text`), the model and the cost of every call, which is
where to look if you have to show how a text was written.

## What is kept, and for how long

The history of a page keeps the brief you wrote, the document and the cost. It
also keeps the full prompt (the brief and what the AI read of the site) so you
can start again from it. How long a prompt is kept is decided in the AI settings
of Thelia; the history follows, and empties the prompts when Thelia empties its
own. The briefs, documents and costs stay.
